Overview

Automating a precision lab task

NPEC runs experiments on Arabidopsis thaliana — a small plant used in biology research. Part of their workflow involves inoculating root tips on Petri dishes with a liquid pipette. It's repetitive, precise, and currently done by hand.

The challenge: build a system that can identify root tips in photos of Petri dishes and drive an OT-2 liquid handling robot to deposit a droplet at each one — automatically, reliably, and without human guidance.

I built the full pipeline: from raw Petri dish images through segmentation, tip extraction, coordinate mapping, all the way to controller execution in a PyBullet physics simulation of the real robot.

The CV Pipeline

From photo to world coordinates

The computer vision pipeline processes each Petri dish image through five stages, running independently on each of the 5 plant strips:

1
Petri dish detection — contour detection finds and crops the dish to a square region
2
U-Net segmentation — 256×256 px patches fed through a trained U-Net, output is a root pixel mask
3
Root extraction — morphological cleaning removes noise; largest connected component per strip = primary root
4
Tip detection — skeleton graph with 8-connectivity; Dijkstra finds longest path; lowest skeleton point = root tip
5
Coordinate transform — pixel coordinates → metres → PyBullet world frame, accounting for the specimen's −90° Z rotation via multiplyTransforms
RL (PPO) controller — trained with Stable Baselines3, 5M timesteps
PID vs RL

Two controllers,
one clear winner

I implemented and compared two controllers. The results were clear — and instructive.

PID — a proportional-integral-derivative controller running independently on X, Y, Z axes. Motion follows three phases per target: lift to safe height → move XY → descend and deposit. Tuned gains achieve sub-millimetre accuracy in 60–800 simulation steps.

PPO (RL) — a Proximal Policy Optimisation agent trained with Stable Baselines3. Observation: [pipette_xyz, goal_xyz]. Action: 3D continuous velocity. Trained for 5 million timesteps using ClearML + Weights & Biases for experiment tracking. Best accuracy: ~5 mm in the CV pipeline integration.

The takeaway: for a well-defined, deterministic, point-to-point task with a known workspace, PID wins — it's faster, more accurate, and requires no training. RL is the right tool when the environment is complex or unpredictable.
